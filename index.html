Giovanni Martinez · fixed-wing operator autonomy in simulation

I’m building a ground panel and flight layer so a non-pilot can run a small fixed-wing without sticks: the aircraft loiters, you tap a target in the camera to Follow, Stop puts it back in loiter, and link-loss sends it home. The interesting part isn’t the tracker branding — it’s making the airplane do the observation geometry after a human pick.

What it does now

  • Operator loop in sim: Launch → Loiter → Select → Follow → Stop → Return
  • Human picks the object; no automatic target acquisition
  • Stationary pick → orbit / standoff framing at altitude
  • Moving pick → trail behind and to the side; work toward matching pace within the airspeed envelope; S-weave when the target is slower than the plane can fly
  • Stock ArduPlane SITL + Isaac camera scene; onboard-style vision track after the tap

Flight controller behaviors (what I’m excited about)

After the tap, the aircraft is supposed to fly the geometry: stabilize, hold altitude, orbit or trail, shape speed without asking the operator for stick inputs. Stop is a first-class abort back to loiter — not “keep diving until something else happens.”

Variable airspeed toward a moving target is in the controller — matching pace when it can, weaving when it can’t hover. Speed-request shaping is still an active experiment (pitch coupling under aggressive accel/brake patterns); I’m treating that as open work, not done.

Where I want it to go

  • Same loop on a real fixed-wing (not just SITL)
  • Cleaner trail / pace match without exciting pitch
  • Human tap in the loop end-to-end, not only scripted Select
  • Later: same operator behaviors on other platform types — FW first
